# Convertible car seats on Target — full comparison & recommendations

**51 convertible car seats** available on Target.com, researched **2026-09-24**. Prices are Target sale prices as of that date.

"Convertible" here means the seat converts rear-facing → forward-facing. That includes 2-in-1 / 3-in-1 / 4-in-1 / all-in-one seats with booster modes, and rotating and non-rotating designs. It excludes infant carriers, harness-to-booster combination seats (forward-facing only) and belt-positioning boosters.

**Read this first:** [Sources & methodology](sources.md) — why "look up the NHTSA safety rating" has no direct answer for most of these seats, and how the 1–5 scores were built.

## ⚠️ Read before you buy anything on this list

**1. NHTSA's official Ease-of-Use rating program covers only 126 seats.** Of the 51 seats here, **14 have a real federal rating** (listed below). The rest do not. Anyone quoting you a "NHTSA star rating" for an unrated seat is quoting something else.

**2. Both Evenflo Revolve360 seats and the Gold variant are under an open federal compliance investigation.** NHTSA investigation **AQ24001**, opened January 5, 2024 and still open: the Revolve360 "appears not to comply with a number of FMVSS No. 213 requirements, including but not limited to the means of installation in S5.3.2 and labeling requirements in S5.5," and NHTSA is "aware of at least six crash incidents in the field during which the Revolve360's shell... reportedly separated from its detachable base." The [Revolve360 Slim](evenflo-revolve360-slim.md) is additionally under recall **25C010000** (324,997 units, headrest foam choking hazard). Evenflo: **1-800-233-5921**.

**3. The Maxi-Cosi Pria platform carries an unremedied recall.** **21C003000** — Pria 85, **119,863 units**, forward-facing lap-belt install without the top tether "may not adequately protect the child from head injury," FMVSS 213 non-compliance. NHTSA's record states **"No remedy has been established at this time."** Current [Pria Pure Cosi](maxicosi-pria-pure-cosi.md) and [Pria Max](maxicosi-pria-max.md) stock is not the recalled seat, but **always use the top tether forward-facing on any Pria.** Dorel: **1-877-657-9546**.

**4. Britax One4Life Slim was recalled for a misstated lower-anchor limit.** **24C001000** — models E1D197A/B/F/G, labels and user guide state the wrong maximum rear-facing lower-anchor weight. Britax: **888-427-4829**. See [One4Life Slim](britax-one4life-slim.md).

**5. Three unrecalled defect patterns worth knowing:**
- [Chicco Fit360](chicco-fit360-cleartex.md) / [Fit360 Zip](chicco-fit360-zip.md) — **26 of 35** federal complaints concern the magnetic chest clip detaching, one mid-drive. Trend rising. No recall.
- [Britax Poplar](britax-poplar.md) — **three of four** federal complaints report the **recline indicator printed backwards**, an airway risk for a newborn. No recall.
- [Evenflo Symphony](evenflo-symphony-extend-breeze.md) — **81 of 102** federal complaints involve the harness or buckle, dominated by **webbing fraying**; two filed by a certified CPST. No current recall.

**6. Check the manufacture date sticker on whatever you buy.** **FMVSS 213a** (side impact) is mandatory only for seats built after **June 30, 2025**. Only 9 of these 51 seats cite 213a on their listing at all.

## Recommendations

**16.7" wide, 21 lb, three modes to 100 lb, steel LockSure belt lock-offs in both directions, explicit FMVSS 213a, GREENGUARD Gold flame-retardant-free fabric, zero complaints, 87% would recommend, no subscore below 4.4.** Nothing under $250 comes close. Owners confirm genuine three-across fitment.

### 🔍 Worth a second look — [Safety 1st EverSlim](safety1st-everslim.md) · $183.99
NHTSA rates this platform **2/5 rear-facing**, which is why it sits mid-table. But **CSFTL calls it "a winner of a seat!"** — 17" at the widest point, **22 lb harnessed and 6 lb backless**, four modes, and it installs "without too much fuss." They were "pleasantly surprised."

The two assessments disagree because they measure different things: NHTSA scored the install *features* and labelling, CSFTL actually installed it and fit-tested it. When a federal rating and the people who take seats apart for a living diverge this far, it is worth knowing both — and this is a better endorsement than the scorecard total suggests.

### ⚖️ Know the trade-off before buying these

**[Chicco Fit360](chicco-fit360-cleartex.md) · $351.99 — the best-protecting rotating seat, and the one with a live defect pattern.** CR rates it crash **Best** with the **highest overall score (84) of any harnessed seat they test**, plus an anti-rebound bar and an explicit FMVSS 213a claim. Against that: **26 of its 35 federal complaints concern the magnetic chest clip detaching**, one mid-drive, the trend is rising, and no recall has been issued. CSFTL also withholds its recommendation over a kick-openable lock-off panel. CR crash-tests one new seat once; it cannot see a clip that degrades after 18 months. Buy it only if you will inspect the clip before every drive — and note it is also the worst cost-per-year here (6-year age cap on an 8-year seat).

**[Britax Galaxy 360](britax-galaxy-360-slim.md) · $343.99 — best hardware on paper, no crash data, worst owner satisfaction.** ClickTight, the only integrated anti-rebound panel here, one-piece seat/base, explicit FMVSS 213a, rear-facing to 50 lb, 17" for three-across. But **Consumer Reports has never tested it**, so the crash margin is unknown, and **29% of owners would recommend it** with a value subscore of 2.73. Buy only if ClickTight, the anti-rebound panel or three-across are specifically what you need, and buy where you can return it.

## Consumer Reports — crash protection

**This is the one dimension nothing else on this page measures.** NHTSA publishes only pass/fail FMVSS 213 compliance; CSFTL does not crash-test. Consumer Reports contracts an independent lab to run **simulated 35-mph frontal crashes** and rates the result **Basic / Better / Best** — that tier is the *margin above the federal floor*, not pass/fail. Every seat sold legally meets FMVSS 213.

CR's other two categories: **Fit to Vehicle** (certified CPSTs install each seat in every unique rear-seat position of five vehicle types, using both LATCH and belt) and **Ease of Use** (instruction clarity, installation, harness adjustment, weight). Overall scores are relative *within seat type*, and CR does not publish the weightings.

Why the data is worth trusting: CR is a nonprofit that **takes no advertising**, **buys every test sample at retail** ("CR staff shoppers buy every product we use as a test sample from retail stores"), and **prohibits companies from using its ratings in advertising**. A good CR score cannot be monetised by the manufacturer, so there is nothing to buy. Contrast CSFTL, whose work is excellent but which does earn Amazon affiliate revenue.

**Source:** paid CR subscriber export, September 25 2026 — `Car Seat Ratings & Reviews - Consumer Reports.pdf` / `.htm`, kept locally and **deliberately not committed** (paid content; see `.gitignore`). CR rates **41 harnessed seats**; 22 of them are seats on this list.

**1. My top pick holds — and for the right reason.** [Graco 4Ever DLX](graco-4ever-dlx.md) is **crash Best**. **Three findings here change the picture from the scorecard above:**

1. **The entire Safety 1st Turn and Go 360 line is CSFTL-unfavourable** — the cheapest rotating-plus-booster option on the list, and the people who take seats apart could not find a reason to buy it. Top harness slot only 15", lowest 8.5", booster belt guide too short to be practical, tippy in booster mode.
2. **[Safety 1st EverSlim](safety1st-everslim.md) is a CSFTL favourite** ("a winner of a seat!", 17" wide, 22 lb harnessed / 6 lb backless) despite NHTSA rating the platform 2/5 rear-facing. Worth more consideration than its scorecard total suggests.
3. **[Britax Poplar](britax-poplar.md) has a vehicle-compatibility catch** the listing never mentions: vehicle headrests fouled the forward-facing install in every car CSFTL tested. If your back seat has fixed headrests, it is out.

31 of 51 seats still have no independent expert assessment. That counts as an evidence gap, not a fault.

## If you take nothing else from these notes

1. **Check the manufacture date sticker.** FMVSS 213a applies by build date (after June 30, 2025). Recall ranges are defined by build date too. Marketing copy tells you nothing.
2. **Register the seat.** Recall notification depends on it — and two of the recalls documented here were themselves for missing registration cards.
3. **Get the install checked by a CPST — it's free.** NHTSA scored **vehicle-installation features 1/5 or 2/5 on seven of the fourteen rated seats here.** That single step outweighs every spec in these notes.
4. **Rear-face as long as the seat allows.** Highest-leverage safety decision available to you, and free.
5. **Always use the top tether forward-facing** — especially on any Maxi-Cosi Pria, where a lap-belt-only forward-facing install is the exact condition of an unremedied 119,863-unit head-injury recall.
6. **Check the Consumer Reports crash-protection tier** for whatever you shortlist. It is the only comparative crash data that exists for US car seats — NHTSA publishes pass/fail only — and Basic / Better / Best measures margin above the federal floor. **29 of these 51 seats have no CR rating at all.**
7. **Don't read ease-of-use ratings as crash protection.** They are unrelated measurements. The Cybex Sirona S has the best rear-facing ease-of-use score in NHTSA's entire dataset and only *Better* crash protection at CR; [Graco 4Ever DLX](graco-4ever-dlx.md) is crash *Best* on a mediocre CR overall of 72.
