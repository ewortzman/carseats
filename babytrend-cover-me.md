## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213. **No FMVSS 213a claim** documented.

**Official NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** (mfg. March 2021):

| Mode | Overall | Securing child | Labels | Vehicle install | Instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rear-facing | **3/5** | **2/5** | **5** | **1/5** | **5** |
| Forward-facing | **4/5** | 4 | **5** | **2/5** | 4 |
| Booster | **3/5** | **2/5** | **1/5** | 3 | **5** |

Labels 5/5 in both harness modes and instructions 5/5 — the paperwork is good. **Vehicle-installation features score 1/5 rear-facing and 2/5 forward-facing**, and **securing the child is 2/5** rear-facing and in booster. Booster labels drop to 1/5.

The NHTSA install scores and the complaint record below point the same direction, which is unusual and makes both more credible.

**All three complaints are about installation, and two are serious:**
- "The seat belt was used first and then switched to the lower anchors... First noticed there was more then an inch of play in the seat after tightening it"
- "When installed per specifications, the seat appears safe. It does not move more than an inch. However, **if even a pound of pressure is applied towards the rear of the vehicle, the seat inverts.** There is an insufficient fulcrum..."
- "Car seat was very difficult to level. The level ball would get stuck out of view, come in to view with adjustments and then go back out of view. **This took place at an inspection station during a car seat check with 2 CPSTs present**"

The second describes the seat rotating out of position under light rearward load — a rebound-control problem. The third was observed by two certified technicians, which makes it hard to dismiss as user error. Together with NHTSA's 1/5 rear-facing install score, this is a consistent picture.

## Cons

- **NHTSA scores vehicle-installation features 1/5 rear-facing, 2/5 forward-facing**
- **2/5 for securing the child** rear-facing and in booster; booster labels **1/5**
- **All three federal complaints are install failures**, including a report that "the seat inverts" under a pound of rearward pressure and one that **two CPSTs could not level**
- No FMVSS 213a claim
- Target price shown only in cart — hard to compare
- No published expiration date
- Does not rotate

## Conclusion

The canopy is a real feature and four modes to 100 lb at a discounted price is real value — but the install evidence is the worst-corroborated on this list. NHTSA scored rear-facing install features 1/5; all three federal complaints are install failures; one describes the seat inverting under light rearward pressure; and one was filed after two CPSTs at an inspection station could not get the level indicator to behave.

That is three independent sources saying the same thing. **Do not buy this seat unless a CPST installs it and confirms it holds**, and if you already own one, get it checked.

For four modes with better install evidence: [Joie Saffron SI](joie-saffron-si.md) $249.99 (213a and 213b, SIP pods, zero complaints) or [Graco 4Ever DLX](graco-4ever-dlx.md) $255.99 (verified 4/5/4, FMVSS 213a, integrated lock-off, 6,173 ratings).
