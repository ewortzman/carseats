## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213. **No FMVSS 213a claim** documented.

**Official NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** — and usefully, this is one of the most recently rated seats in the dataset (mfg. **June 2025**), so it reflects current production rather than a years-old build:

| Mode | Overall | Securing child | Labels | Vehicle install | Instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rear-facing | **3/5** | 3 | **5** | **1/5** | **5** |
| Forward-facing | **3/5** | 3 | **5** | **2/5** | 3 |

The earlier **Baby Trend Trooper** (mfg. Dec 2020) scored 3/5 in both modes with 2/5 install features both ways — so the Slim's rear-facing install score got *worse*, from 2/5 to 1/5.

Labels 5/5 in both modes and rear-facing instructions 5/5 are good. **1/5 rear-facing vehicle-installation features** is the problem, and it is the same weakness NHTSA found on the [Cover Me](babytrend-cover-me.md) (also 1/5 rear-facing).

**Forward-facing lower-anchor limit of 60 lb is the highest in this comparison** — you can stay on LATCH far longer than with most seats.

Note the **40" rear-facing height cap** — the shortest in this comparison alongside [Safety 1st Grow and Go](safety1st-grow-and-go.md) and [Maxi-Cosi Romi](maxicosi-romi.md). Many children hit 40" around age 3.5–4, well before 40 lb.

## Reliability & owner feedback

4.76★ from 82 ratings — **the highest-rated Baby Trend in this comparison** and among the highest averages on the whole list.

Target does not publish attribute subscores on this listing, and the price displays only in cart.

The gap between 4.76★ from owners and 3/5 from NHTSA is the familiar pattern: owners rate comfort, looks and perceived ease; NHTSA measures whether the hardware and labelling make a correct install likely.

**CSFTL: favourable.** They highlight how unusual it is to get a light, slim seat that fits preemies through a typical 7-year-old for under $100, calling it **"an excellent option for many situations, especially tight 3 across setups"** and suggesting it would suit air travel too. Their measurements: **17.5" at the widest point** (with cupholder attached, at the shoulders) and **10 lb** — by far the lightest seat in this comparison. Drawbacks: required harness covers poked their younger testers' necks; **the seat tends to tip toward the buckle in seat-belt installs because it is so light**; harness-slot and crotch-buckle rules are complicated enough to require the manual; the continuous harness complicates evening out the low-birthweight routing; and the manual gives three seemingly conflicting statements about seat belt vs. LATCH installation.

## Conclusion

Better than the [Cover Me](babytrend-cover-me.md) — clean complaint record, 4.76★, and labels and rear-facing instructions both 5/5. But NHTSA scored rear-facing install features **1/5** on a 2025 build, which is the single most important axis and the one Baby Trend keeps failing.

The 40" rear-facing height cap is the other limiter: extended rear-facing is the best safety decision available to you, and this seat takes it away early.

If the in-cart price lands under $150, it is a reasonable budget seat **provided a CPST installs it**. Above that, [Chicco Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) at $183.99 gives you 16.7", steel lock-offs both directions, an explicit 213a claim, dual bubble levels and rear-facing to 43" — with none of the 1/5 scores.
