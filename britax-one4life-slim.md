## Safety

**FMVSS 213a cited** on the One4Life line, with Britax disclosing that 213a covers harnessed children only to 40 lb / 43" and not booster mode.

Steel frame with SafeCell, two energy-management layers, patented V-shaped tether. **No anti-rebound bar listed.**

**No NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** for this model. The **Britax Poplar** is rated (mfg. June 2024) and is the nearest proxy:

| Mode | Overall | Securing child | Labels | Vehicle install | Instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rear-facing | **4/5** | 4 | **5** | 3 | 4 |
| Forward-facing | **3/5** | 4 | **5** | **2/5** | **2/5** |

Britax's brand pattern: strong labels, weak install features and forward-facing instructions.

Rear-facing to 50 lb with a 120 lb booster ceiling in a narrower shell is the reason this variant exists.

## NHTSA record

| Item | Count |
|---|---|

| Recalls | **1 — 24C001000** |

**Recall 24C001000** (received Nov 19, 2024): "Britax is recalling certain One4Life Slim ClickTight All-in-One car seats with model numbers E1D197A, E1D197B, E1D197F, and E1D197G. The labels and user guide **incorrectly state the maximum rear-facing lower anchor weight**. As such, these child seats fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 213." 1,286 units. Britax mailed new labels free of charge; owner letters from December 18, 2024. Owners: **888-427-4829**.

A misstated lower-anchor weight limit means a caregiver could keep using lower anchors past the safe load. That is an install failure, not a paperwork one.

Complaint themes: install/LATCH 4 · harness 3 · breakage 1.

**Two separate filings report a child's arm becoming trapped in the forward-facing belt path while rear-facing:**
- "my son is rear facing and puts his arm in the forward facing belt path then can not get his arm back out. he starts screaming and crying and i have to pull the car over"
- "We were driving on the freeway when my son started screaming to a point we had to get over and stop... His arm got stuck in the forward facing belt path and he couldn't get it out."

Also filed: "The tension release at the bottom of the harness... has a screw holding it to the shell of the seat. This screw b[roke]" — a structural failure on the harness tensioner.


## Reliability & owner feedback

**3.95★ from 52 ratings — the lowest star average of any seat in this comparison** (a duplicate listing shows 4.0 from the same 52 ratings).

## Cons

- **Named in recall 24C001000** — incorrect maximum rear-facing lower-anchor weight on labels and user guide, FMVSS 213 non-compliance
- **3.95★ — lowest star average in this comparison**, on only 52 ratings
- Harness extremely hard to tighten
- Cover separates from the shell
- Two reports of a child's arm trapped in the forward-facing belt path
- Broken harness-tensioner screw reported
- **No anti-rebound bar**
- **Same $359.99 as the full-size version** despite the smaller shell
- No published expiration

## Conclusion

Skip it. It costs the same as the full-size [One4Life](britax-one4life.md), rates lower (3.95★ vs 4.03★), and is the variant that got recalled for misprinting the rear-facing lower-anchor limit.

For a narrow Britax with ClickTight and 50 lb rear-facing, buy [Britax Poplar](britax-poplar.md) at $307.99 — 17" wide, 4.65★, 1% one-star, no recall. You lose the booster modes.

For four modes to 120 lb with a strong record, [Graco 4Ever DLX](graco-4ever-dlx.md) at $255.99 beats it on every axis except rear-facing weight.

If you own one: check your model number against **E1D197A/B/F/G** and call Britax at 888-427-4829 for corrected labels.
