## Safety

Certification listed is **FMVSS 213 only — no FMVSS 213a claim** on this listing, unlike [Galaxy 360](britax-galaxy-360-slim.md) and [One4Life](britax-one4life.md), which both cite it. Check the manufacture date.

Structural claims: steel frame, **SafeCell crumple zone**, and a patented V-shaped top tether to manage crash energy. **No anti-rebound bar** is included — a reviewer notes the seat is "set up so you can add the stability bar," so it is an accessory purchase.

**Official NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** (mfg. June 2024):

Labels score 5/5 in both modes — Britax's brand strength. But forward-facing scores **2/5 on vehicle-installation features and 2/5 on instructions**, which is Britax's documented brand weakness and matches the owner complaints below.

Rear-facing to **50 lb / 49"** is among the best in this comparison, matching [Galaxy 360](britax-galaxy-360-slim.md), [UPPAbaby Rove](uppababy-rove.md) and the Evenflo rotating seats.

## NHTSA record

| Item | Count |
|---|---|
| Complaints | **4** |
| Injuries | 0 |
| Recalls | **0** |
| Open investigations | **0** |

Themes: **recline 3** · install 2 · crash 1 · harness 1 · breakage 1 · fabric 1.

**Three of the four complaints report a reversed or incorrect recline indicator:**
- "Car seat received brand new with incorrect level indicator. The portion designed to show maximum recline is on opposite side and actually results in maximum upright position."
- "There is a problem with the recline indicator, as the actual recline of the seat works opposite of the manual's instruction. Page 18 in the manual indicates that for a newborn, you need to put the seat in the 'most reclined position'..."
- "The recline indicator is incorrect. When all the way upright the ball is in the rear facing only section."

This matters. A newborn without head control placed in the *most upright* position because the indicator reads backwards is an airway risk. No recall has been issued. Compare: Evenflo was recalled twice in 2026 (**25C011000**, **26C005000**) for exactly this class of defect — a recline mechanism or indicator that misleads the caregiver.

**If you buy this seat, verify the recline against the vehicle-floor-parallel check and have a CPST confirm it — do not trust the indicator alone.**

## Reliability & owner feedback

4.65★ from 99 ratings; 74% five-star, 20% four, 4% three, 1% two, **1% one**. Target does not publish attribute subscores here.

That distribution is the healthiest of any Britax in this comparison — [Galaxy 360](britax-galaxy-360-slim.md) has 15% one-star and [One4Life](britax-one4life.md) 16%.

Praise: "The ClickTight installation system is a game-changer"; using the seatbelt is "less intimidating than the lower anchors"; extended rear-facing — "our 4.5 year old is still comfortably rear facing in hers"; comfort and padding; footprint — "Very slim side to side and incredibly compact front to back."

Complaints:
- **Cleaning and reassembly is the dominant gripe** — "washing the covers and putting it back on is nearly impossible"; strap pads are not removable, so "we cannot wash the smell out of them."
- Harness adjuster release "is sometimes hard for me to find/reach"; the tightening strap sits against the vehicle seat.
- Squeeze-style LATCH connectors disliked — "the incredibly frustrating squeeze latch thing."
- Top-tether tightening angle "really difficult."
- ClickTight described as "very temperamental compared to our other click tight car seats."


**CSFTL: positive, with one vehicle-compatibility warning the listing never mentions.** They call it "a solid addition to Britax's lineup" that "really shines in rear facing mode," praising how compact it is upright rear-facing, its narrow profile and ClickTight. **But vehicle headrests interfered with the forward-facing install in every test vehicle, so CSFTL does not recommend it for cars with non-removable headrests.** They also flag that it is "not the longest-lasting option for extended rear facing, nor does it offer a ton of legroom," and its curvier shape caused trouble in narrow centre positions. Their measurements: **17" at the headrest, 16.5" at the cupholder area with cupholders off — but 20" once they are installed**; 13.5" base; **26 lb**.

## Conclusion

The Britax to buy if you want a Britax. ClickTight, 50 lb rear-facing, 17" for three-across, a steel frame with a crumple zone, and — unlike [Galaxy 360](britax-galaxy-360-slim.md) and [One4Life](britax-one4life.md) — owners actually like it. At $307.99 it undercuts both.

Two conditions. First, **check the recline indicator against the manual and have a CPST verify the angle**, because three of four federal complaints say the indicator is printed backwards and Britax has not recalled it. Second, accept that washing it is genuinely painful and the strap pads cannot be removed at all.

If you would rather not manage a possibly-wrong recline indicator, [Chicco Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) at $183.99 gives you 16.7", steel lock-offs, an explicit 213a claim, dual bubble levels and three modes — at the cost of rear-facing stopping at 40 lb.
