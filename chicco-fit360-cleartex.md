# Chicco Fit360 ClearTex Rotating Convertible Car Seat

**Price:** $351.99 (reg. $419.99) · **Target TCIN** 94371984 · [Back to summary](README.md)
**Target product page:** [https://www.target.com/p/chicco-fit360-cleartex-rotating-convertible-car-seat-obsidian/-/A-94371984](https://www.target.com/p/chicco-fit360-cleartex-rotating-convertible-car-seat-obsidian/-/A-94371984)

## Verdict

**Best-feeling seat in the group, undermined by a live chest-clip failure pattern.** Highest comfort and installation subscores of any rotating seat here, a genuine belt lock-off, and a second-base option. But 26 of its 35 NHTSA complaints are about the magnetic chest clip coming apart — including while driving — and the complaint rate is accelerating. No recall has been issued. Also the shortest usable life and worst cost-per-year in the comparison.

## Safety

- Meets FMVSS 213. Chicco claims side-impact protection; the product page does not explicitly cite **FMVSS 213a** the way [Britax Galaxy 360](britax-galaxy-360-slim.md) and [Graco Turn2Me](graco-turn2me.md) do. Check the manufacture date sticker.
- **No NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating exists** for the Fit360. Chicco seats that *are* rated, for brand calibration:
  - **Chicco OneFit ClearTex** (all-in-one): RF overall **3/5**, FF **3/5**, booster **3/5** — weak labels (2/5 RF) and weak vehicle-installation features (2/5 both harness modes)
  - **Chicco Fit4** (all-in-one): RF **4/5**, FF **4/5**, booster **3/5** — but labels scored **1/5** forward-facing and in booster
  - Pattern: Chicco scores well on instructions, poorly on labels. Not directly transferable, but it is the brand's documented tendency.
- Tether required in **both** directions is a genuine safety plus — most convertibles only require it forward-facing.

**Complaint breakdown:**

| Subject | Count |
|---|---|
| Magnetic chest clip / magnet | **26 of 35** |
| Recline / install / belt path | 4 |

**Trend:** 12 complaints in 2025 → **23 in the first nine months of 2026**. Rising, not decaying.

Representative filings:
- "the magnetic chest clip easily detaches if even slight pressure is applied and... the two buttons do not need to be pressed in order for the clip to come apart"
- "failing to stay clipped and is easily unclipped by the tug of one finger. Children are able to easily unclip it as well"
- "While driving, the chest clip fell apart into multiple little pieces. I was not aware until we were parked at our destination"
- "The base of the seat where the top part of the belt path goes is bent... now the base is broken"

**Independent corroboration:** Target reviews show the same issue as the #1 complaint theme — "The magnetic chest clip has already broken. It will not stay clipped anymore," with one owner reporting repeat failure over two years. Chicco's posted replies direct people to customer service rather than acknowledging a pattern.

Two independent datasets converging on the same component is a real defect signature, not noise. No recall has been issued as of September 2026.

## Reliability

- 4.47★ from 350 ratings; **78% would recommend**.
- Subscores: comfort 4.64, installation 4.62, quality 4.47, durability 4.47, value 4.44. All above 4.4 — the strongest subscore profile of any rotating seat here.
- Distribution 80% five-star / **8% one-star** — an unusually heavy one-star tail for a 4.5 average, consistent with a component that fails for a minority rather than a seat that disappoints broadly.

## Ease of use

**Car Seats for the Littles (CSFTL) findings:**
- "Very easy install, simple rotation," second-base option, crash-tested mirror accessory, ClearTex fabric, dishwasher-safe cupholders.
- Fit: 8 lb / 20" live newborn and a 7 lb doll both fit well **with inserts**; without inserts straps sat above the shoulders, which is not permitted rear-facing. A 43" / 42 lb 4.5-year-old still had room.
- The 18" top harness slot beats the typical 17" — good for tall kids.
- A 4.5-year-old tester **could not** open the magnetic chest clip, and it resists sliding out of position. (Note the tension with the NHTSA filings — CSFTL tested a clip that worked; the complaints describe clips that degrade in service.)

**CSFTL criticisms:**
- **A barefoot 3-year-old used her feet to partially open the lock-off panel.** Chicco says a locked seat cannot lose belt tension; CSFTL calls it "a big deal" and advises against the seat for curious, long-legged, shoeless rear-facing kids.
- Early units had a sticking lock-off (reportedly fixed in retail stock).
- The 6-year age cap undercuts the tall 18" harness height; CSFTL wants it removed.
- The under-11-lb insert requirement came from customer service, not the manual.
- Heavy for travel despite FAA approval.
- **CSFTL has not placed it on its recommended list** pending resolution of the lock-off issue.

**Owner friction:**
- Rotation not always one-handed: "It does not rotate facing forward easily at all. I really have to force it"; another said the swivel "requires two hands!?"
- Cover removal/reinstallation is a recurring gripe on the non-zip version; one owner rated ease of cleaning 1/5. (The Fit360 **Zip** at $440.99 addresses this and rates 4.83★, but costs $89 more.)
- 32" front-to-back pushes front seats far forward, even in a three-row SUV.
- A child can pull out the cup holders.

## Pros

- Best comfort and installation scores of any rotating seat here.
- Genuine built-in lock-off (LeverLock) plus shoulder belt guide — rare on a convertible, and better than the "tensioning arm" on [Revolve360 Slim](evenflo-revolve360-slim.md)/[Extend](evenflo-revolve360-extend.md), which CSFTL says "is not a true lockoff."
- Tether required both directions.
- 18" top harness slot — taller than most.
- Second base available, so the seat moves between two cars without reinstalling.
- FAA certified.
- Base installs the same way for both directions — potentially a one-time install.

## Cons

- **26 NHTSA complaints on the magnetic chest clip, accelerating, no recall.**
- CSFTL will not recommend it — kick-openable lock-off panel.
- Shortest life here: 8-year expiry *and* a 6-year age cap, so effectively 6 years of use.
- Worst cost-per-year in the comparison at $58.66.
- No booster mode.
- 32" front-to-back — largest footprint here.
- Rotation stiff forward-facing for some owners.
- Cover removal is painful unless you pay up for the Zip version.
- Extra base is $189.99 — expensive.

## Brand track record — Chicco

From the full NHTSA dataset (17 Chicco model records, 16 active):

- **178 total complaints, 8 reported injuries, 2 distinct recalls, 0 investigations** — brand-wide, all history.
- Smallest US catalogue of the four major brands here, and a correspondingly small compliance footprint.
- Most recent recall: **25C005000** (July 2025) — MyFit Zip Air harness/booster seats, "may allow excessive chest movement in a crash," fails FMVSS 213, 30,984 units. Remedy kit mailed free. **Does not affect the Fit360.**
- Zero investigations in the brand's history — better than [Graco](graco-turn2me.md) (3), [Britax](britax-galaxy-360-slim.md) (6), and far better than [Evenflo](evenflo-revolve360-slim.md) (25).
- Chicco's brand record is good. The Fit360's specific chest-clip pattern is the outlier, not the norm.

## Conclusion

This is the nicest seat here to live with day to day, and if the magnetic chest clip were a standard clip it would be a straightforward runner-up pick. It is not a standard clip, and 26 owners have told the federal government it comes apart — one of them mid-drive, without the caregiver noticing until they parked.

Recommendation: **hold off.** The clip is the component that keeps the harness positioned over the child's shoulders in a crash; a degrading clip is not a cosmetic defect. Combined with CSFTL declining to recommend it over the kick-openable lock-off, and with the worst cost-per-year in the group ($58.66) driven by a 6-year age cap, there is no metric on which it clearly beats [Graco EasyTurn 360](graco-easyturn-360.md) at $72 less.

Revisit if Chicco redesigns the clip or issues a remedy. If you buy it anyway: inspect the clip before every drive, and file with NHTSA if it fails — the complaint volume is what triggers a recall investigation.
