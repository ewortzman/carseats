## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213; Chicco claims side-impact protection. The listing does not name **FMVSS 213a** the way [Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) and [OneFit Max](chicco-onefit-max.md) do — check the manufacture date sticker.

**Official NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** (OneFit ClearTex, mfg. Oct 2023):

| Mode | Overall | Securing child | Labels | Vehicle install | Instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rear-facing | **3/5** | 4 | **2/5** | **2/5** | 5 |
| Forward-facing | **3/5** | 4 | 3 | **2/5** | 4 |
| Booster | **3/5** | **1/5** | 5 | 3 | 4 |

3/5 across all three modes is the weakest overall profile of any Chicco or Graco seat in this comparison, and **1/5 for securing the child in booster mode** is near the floor of the scale. Vehicle-installation features score 2/5 in both harness modes.

The one standout spec is **forward-facing to 54"** — five inches taller than the 49" most seats here allow, which buys real harnessed time for a tall child.

Representative filings:
- "[child] is able to press the buckle button and chest clip button and completely free themselves from the car seat. Buckle button does not meet federal safety regulations of required force to release buckle button and is easily..."
- "I took the cover off our car seat to wash and the foam on the headrest was cracked all the way through... This car seat is barely 2 years old."
- "Our seat cover will not cover part of the head rest and he is able to pick and scratch at the Styrofoam. **Even caught him trying to eat it.** ... We have had to stop using the seat"

The headrest-foam exposure theme appears in 6 of 8 filings. This is the same failure class that triggered Evenflo's 324,997-unit recall **25C010000** on the [Revolve360 Slim](evenflo-revolve360-slim.md) — Chicco has not been recalled for it.

## Reliability & owner feedback

4.57★ from 96 ratings. Full subscores not published on this listing (a separate OneFit LX listing at $319.99 shows 4.65★/54).

Praise: roomy for tall children, long forward-facing runway, plush padding, ClearTex fabric.

Complaints mirror the NHTSA themes: exposed/cracking headrest foam, cover removal difficulty, and a buckle some children can release themselves.

**CSFTL: favourable.** The seat "impressed us in many ways," accommodating children from newborn through upper elementary years. They single out that **most multimode seats fall short of a true ten-year lifespan because the booster belt guide sits too low for bigger kids, whereas this one delivers** — a meaningful distinction given how many seats here have nominal booster ceilings. Lockoffs, SuperCinch LATCH and the no-rethread harness reportedly "make installing and using the child restraint a breeze." Their measurements: **19.25" at the shoulders, 21.5" with the cupholders extended**, **24 lb** assembled ("kind of a heavy seat," not travel-friendly). Minor drawbacks: the two-position chest clip is puzzling to set correctly, and the seat's width kept their 5- and 8-year-old models from buckling themselves in booster mode.

## Pros

- **Forward-facing to 54"** — longest harnessed height limit of any seat in this comparison
- Three modes to 100 lb
- Belt lock-off present
- ClearTex flame-retardant-free fabric
- No recalls or investigations
- 4.57★

## Cons

- **NHTSA rates it 3/5 in all three modes**, with 2/5 labels rear-facing, 2/5 vehicle-install features in both harness modes, and **1/5 securing the child in booster**
- **6 of 8 NHTSA complaints are cracked or exposed headrest foam**, with one child caught eating it
- A filing alleges the buckle release force does not meet federal requirements and children can free themselves
- No FMVSS 213a claim on the listing
- $280.49 — $96.50 more than the better-specified [Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md)
- 19.5"-class width; not a three-across seat
- Does not rotate

## Conclusion

Hard to justify. It costs $96.50 more than [Chicco Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md), which is narrower (16.7" vs ~19.5"), lighter, carries an explicit FMVSS 213a claim, has steel lock-offs in both directions, and has zero complaints — while this seat is the one Chicco convertible NHTSA actually measured, and it scored 3/5 across the board.

Buy it only for the **54" forward-facing height limit**, which is the longest here and genuinely useful for a very tall child who has outgrown 49" seats but isn't ready for a booster.

If you buy it: pull the cover before first use and inspect the headrest foam. Six of eight federal complaints are about that foam cracking or being exposed where a child can pick at it, and one parent reported their child eating it.
