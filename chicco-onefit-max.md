## Verdict

The roomiest Chicco convertible here — built for tall and heavy children, with a **SuperCinch force-multiplying tightener**, a **LeverLock self-tensioning lock-off**, 8 recline positions and an explicit **FMVSS 213a** claim. Zero NHTSA complaints. But it is 19.5" × 23.25", 27 lb, $341.99, and only 49 ratings — and owners report a stiff harness and a magnetic clip that sticks.

## Safety

**Cites FMVSS 213a explicitly** — "Advanced Side Impact Protection (ASIP)," engineered to meet "FMVSS 213a – Side Impact." Steel-reinforced shell, EPP energy-managing foam.

Two genuinely good install mechanisms: **SuperCinch** multiplies your pulling force on the lower anchors rear-facing, and **LeverLock** is a true self-tensioning lock-off forward-facing. Together they address the exact weakness NHTSA scored 2/5 on the [OneFit LX](chicco-onefit-lx.md) (vehicle-installation features). 8 recline positions is the most of any seat in this comparison.

**No NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** for the OneFit Max. Nearest rated Chicco is the **OneFit ClearTex** at 3/3/3 — see [OneFit LX](chicco-onefit-lx.md). Chicco's brand pattern is good instructions, weak labels.

Note the magnetic chest clip: the same component type behind **26 of 35** NHTSA complaints on the [Chicco Fit360](chicco-fit360-cleartex.md). No Fit360-style complaint pattern has emerged here yet, but this seat has only 49 ratings — far too little exposure to conclude the clip is fine. Inspect it regularly.

## Reliability & owner feedback

4.70★ from 49 ratings; 84% five-star, 0% one-star. Target's recommend metric displays anomalously ("0% would recommend" from 1 recommendation) — disregard it. Subscores appear on a single review only: **ease of installation 2/5**, everything else 4/5.

Praise: roomy for tall and heavier babies, generous rear-facing legroom, plush padding, soft fabric, and the magnetic clip — one parent called it "a game changer." Several found the install straightforward.

Complaints: stiff harness that is "really hard" to adjust; a magnetic clip that "keeps sticking"; bulk that barely fit a Ford Explorer; difficult belt install with a bubble level that "never stays in place"; no zip-off cover; harness adjuster strap slipping under the fabric.

## Pros

- **FMVSS 213a cited explicitly**
- **SuperCinch force-multiplying tightener** rear-facing plus a true **LeverLock self-tensioning lock-off** forward-facing — best install hardware of any Chicco here
- **8 recline positions** — most in the comparison
- Roomiest Chicco; generous rear-facing legroom for tall/heavy children
- Forward-facing to 54"
- GREENGUARD Gold, flame-retardant-free
- Zero NHTSA complaints, recalls, investigations

## Cons

- **$341.99** — you can buy [Chicco Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) plus most of a second seat for that
- **19.5" × 23.25" and 27 lb** — bulky; barely fit a Ford Explorer per one owner
- Magnetic chest clip — the component behind 26 of 35 complaints on the [Fit360](chicco-fit360-cleartex.md); owners here already report it sticking
- Stiff harness, hard to adjust
- Bubble level "never stays in place"
- Only 49 ratings — almost no field evidence
- No zip-off cover
- No published expiration date
- Does not rotate

## Conclusion

Genuinely good engineering — SuperCinch plus LeverLock is the best install hardware Chicco offers, 8 reclines is the most here, and the 213a claim is explicit. If you have a tall or heavy child and a large vehicle, it fits them better than anything else Chicco sells.

But at $341.99 it costs more than [Graco EasyTurn 360](graco-easyturn-360.md) (which rotates) and nearly double [Chicco Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) (which is narrower, lighter, also 213a-certified, also lock-off equipped, and has a non-magnetic harness). With 49 ratings there is not enough field data to know whether the magnetic clip behaves better here than it does on the [Fit360](chicco-fit360-cleartex.md).

Buy [Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) unless your child specifically needs the extra room.
