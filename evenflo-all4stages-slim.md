## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213; JPMA certified. **No FMVSS 213a claim** documented on the listing — check the manufacture date.

**No NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** for this model. Evenflo's rated seats, for brand calibration:

| Seat | RF overall | FF overall | Booster overall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evenflo EveryFit | 4/5 | **5/5** | 3/5 (securing child **1/5**) |
| Evenflo All4One | 4/5 | 4/5 | 3/5 (securing child **1/5**, install **1/5**) |

Evenflo scores respectably in harness modes and consistently badly in booster mode.

The booster pattern matters here: this is a 4-mode seat whose last two stages are booster modes, and that is precisely where Evenflo's rated seats score 3/5 overall with **1/5 for securing the child**.

| Item | Count |

| Recalls | **0** |

The single filing: "Whenever I use the harness release and re-tighten the harness, the back security ring comes off the harness. It's happened multiple times and I have to rethread the harness and rehook it to the ring." — a harness splitter plate detaching in normal use. One report, but it is a load-path component.

By Evenflo standards this is a clean record, and it is worth crediting: the brand's problems are concentrated in the Revolve and Symphony platforms, not universal.

Evenflo brand-wide: **3,541 complaints, 270 injuries, 41 recalls, 25 investigations** across 321 model records — the worst of any brand in this comparison on every normalisation. Eight recalls since 2023 covering over 547,000 units, most for FMVSS 213 non-compliance. Full table on the [Revolve360 Slim page](evenflo-revolve360-slim.md#brand-track-record--evenflo).

Two patterns worth carrying: Evenflo's **All4One scored 4/5 rear-facing from NHTSA and was later recalled** (25C011000) for a rear-facing recline mechanism that shifts out of position — so a good ease-of-use score does not predict compliance. And Evenflo's remedies lag peers: mailed tape for a 325,000-unit foam recall, versus [Graco](graco-turn2me.md) replacing seats outright for 26C003000.

## Pros

- **4 modes, 4 lb to 120 lb** — full span in one seat
- Only **1 NHTSA complaint**, zero recalls, zero investigations — genuinely clean for an Evenflo
- Slim shell
- Built-in sun canopy, unusual and useful for a rear-facing baby
- $259.99 for four modes

## Cons

- 4.29★ — below every Graco and Chicco convertible here
- Evenflo's rated all-in-ones score **3/5 in booster with 1/5 securing the child**, and booster is two of this seat's four modes
- **No FMVSS 213a claim** documented
- Never rated by NHTSA
- **90-day warranty**
- A filing reports the harness splitter ring detaching repeatedly
- Evenflo brand record is the worst here (41 recalls, 25 investigations)
- Does not rotate

## Conclusion

The best-behaved Evenflo convertible in this comparison, which is a low bar but a real distinction — 1 complaint versus 102 on the [Symphony](evenflo-symphony-extend-breeze.md) and 21–41 across the [Revolve360](evenflo-revolve360-slim.md) family. The canopy is genuinely useful and four modes to 120 lb is full coverage.

Still hard to recommend over [Joie Saffron SI](joie-saffron-si.md) at $249.99, which gives you four modes to 120 lb, **FMVSS 213a and 213b** compliance, SIP pods, a LockTight belt tensioner, 88% would-recommend and 4.49★ — for $10 less and a much better brand record.

If you want this seat for the canopy, buy it and use the booster modes with care: check the belt fit across your child's hips and shoulder yourself rather than trusting the seat's guides.
