## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213. **No FMVSS 213a claim.** Steel-reinforced frame.

**No NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating.** See [Graco 4Ever DLX](graco-4ever-dlx.md) (4/5/4) and [TriRide](graco-triride-3in1.md) (4/4/4) for brand calibration.

Rear-facing to 50 lb with an extending leg-room panel is the headline safety feature and it is a real one — extended rear-facing is the single highest-leverage safety choice available to a caregiver.

Same pattern as the [2-in-1](graco-extend2fit-2in1.md): the forward-facing harness is hard to tighten and the LATCH hardware draws complaints.

## Pros

- **Rear-facing to 50 lb** with extending leg-room panel
- Adds highback booster to 100 lb over the [2-in-1](graco-extend2fit-2in1.md)
- 4.67★ from 1,466 ratings
- 10-year life
- $202.49 — cheaper than every rotating seat here

## Cons

- 13 NHTSA complaints; forward-facing harness tightening and LATCH hardware are the repeat themes
- No FMVSS 213a claim
- Never rated by NHTSA
- Exposed headrest foam reported across the family
- Does not rotate

## Conclusion

The better of the two Extend2Fit listings — you get booster mode for $22.50 more. Worth it if extended rear-facing to 50 lb is what you came for.

Against [Graco 4Ever DLX](graco-4ever-dlx.md) at $255.99: the 4Ever gives you a verified 4/5/4 NHTSA rating, an FMVSS 213a claim, a belt lock-off, a fourth mode to 120 lb, and 6,173 ratings — but rear-facing stops at 40 lb. Pick on that single axis: if you want to rear-face past 40 lb, buy this; otherwise buy the 4Ever.
