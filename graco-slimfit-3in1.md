## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213. **No FMVSS 213a claim** on the listing — check the manufacture date.

**Official NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** (mfg. June 2024) — and it is the weakest install profile in this comparison:

| Mode | Overall | Securing child | Labels | Vehicle install | Instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rear-facing | 4/5 | 5 | 3 | **1/5** | 5 |
| Forward-facing | 4/5 | 5 | 3 | **2/5** | 3 |
| Booster | 4/5 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 |

A **1/5 on rear-facing vehicle-installation features** is close to the floor of NHTSA's scale. Securing the child scores 5/5 in both harness modes, so the harness itself is fine — it is getting the seat correctly attached to the car that NHTSA penalised. That is the failure that matters most.

Rear-facing to 49" is unusually generous for a 40 lb weight cap, so tall-but-light children get extra rear-facing time.

One complaint on a 2,169-rating seat is a genuinely low rate.

## Reliability & owner feedback

4.71★ from 2,169 ratings. Target does not publish full subscores on this listing.

Widely bought and well liked; the general owner profile mirrors the [SlimFit3 LX](graco-slimfit3-lx.md) — praised for narrow fitment and easy cleaning, criticised for recline geometry and tight rear-facing legroom.

## Pros

- Rear-facing to **49"** on a 40 lb cap — long rear-facing runway for tall, light children
- 4.71★ across 2,169 ratings with only 1 NHTSA complaint
- 3 modes to 100 lb, 10 years
- Narrow, marketed for three-across
- $172.49

## Cons

- **NHTSA scored rear-facing vehicle-installation features 1/5** and forward-facing 2/5 — the worst install-feature scores in this comparison
- Forward-facing instructions 3/5
- No FMVSS 213a claim
- Superseded by the [SlimFit3 LX](graco-slimfit3-lx.md), which adds a forward-facing lock-off
- Does not rotate

## Conclusion

Pick the [SlimFit3 LX](graco-slimfit3-lx.md) over this one. It costs $52 more and adds a forward-facing belt lock-off, a documented 16.7" width, 19.2 lb, and a 4.63 install subscore — directly addressing the thing NHTSA scored 1/5 here.

The one reason to prefer this seat: rear-facing to 49" gives a tall, light child more rear-facing time than most seats on this list. If that is your situation and the price matters, it is defensible — but get the install checked by a CPST, because NHTSA's scoring says the install features are this seat's weakest point.
