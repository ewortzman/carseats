Securing the child scores 5/5 in both harness modes — the harness geometry is good. **Vehicle-installation features score 1/5 rear-facing and 2/5 forward-facing, and labels 2/5 and 1/5.** Getting the seat correctly attached to the car is the documented weakness.

Maxi-Cosi is a **Dorel Juvenile Group** brand, as are Safety 1st and the Disney Baby car seat line. Every rated Dorel convertible or all-in-one in this comparison scores **2/5 or 3/5 rear-facing overall**: [Maxi-Cosi Romi](maxicosi-romi.md) 3/5, [Pria All-in-One](maxicosi-pria-pure-cosi.md) 3/5, [Safety 1st Grow and Go](safety1st-grow-and-go.md) 2/5, [EverSlim](safety1st-everslim.md) 2/5, [Trimate](safety1st-trimate.md) 2/5. Four of the five score **1/5 on rear-facing vehicle-installation features**.

Dorel recall history in this comparison: **21C003000** (Maxi-Cosi Pria 85, 119,863 units, potential head injury with lap-belt use, **"No remedy has been established at this time"**) and **21C004000** (Pria 70, 4,000 units, same defect — remedied with replacement labels plus a free booster seat).

Clean on its own record. The Pria **family** records carry 11 complaints and two recalls:

**Recall 21C003000** — Maxi-Cosi **Pria 85**: installed forward-facing with a lap belt and no top tether, "the seat may not adequately protect the child from head injury." FMVSS 213 non-compliance. **119,863 units.** NHTSA: **"No remedy has been established at this time."** Owner letters October 8, 2021. Dorel: 1-877-657-9546.

Neither covers the current Pria Max. Family complaint themes include **harness webbing fraying near the hip** and **foam breaking in half** out of the box. Detail on the [Pria Pure Cosi page](maxicosi-pria-pure-cosi.md).

## Reliability & owner feedback

4.08★ from 383 ratings. Target publishes no attribute subscores on this listing.

383 ratings makes 4.08 a meaningful figure, and it is low: below [Graco 4Ever DLX](graco-4ever-dlx.md) (4.78/6,173), [Chicco Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) (4.62/127), [Joie Saffron SI](joie-saffron-si.md) (4.49/147) and [Graco SlimFit3 LX](graco-slimfit3-lx.md) (4.74/1,363) — all of which cost less.

**CSFTL: positive, with reservations.** They call it "a plush seat that fits our newborn and preemie dolls well" and say installation is "far easier than on the previous model," crediting Maxi-Cosi for details like harness covers and small-infant positioning. Their measurements: **20" at the widest point** (cupholders), **18 lb**. Downsides: notably short top-tether webbing, a chest clip that must be stored separately in booster mode, and **limited forward-facing longevity** — their 4-year-old was already near the top harness setting despite being 6" under the height limit, so another harnessed seat would likely be needed. The wide base also made self-buckling hard in booster mode in one test vehicle.

## Conclusion

No. At $329.99 you are paying a premium for 4.08★, a platform with an unremedied 119,863-unit head-injury recall on its name, and a NHTSA rating of 3/5 across the board with 1/5 rear-facing install features.

[Joie Saffron SI](joie-saffron-si.md) at $249.99 gives four modes to 120 lb, FMVSS 213a *and* 213b, physical SIP pods, zero complaints, the cleanest brand record here, 88% would recommend and comfort 4.89 — for $80 less. [Graco 4Ever DLX](graco-4ever-dlx.md) at $255.99 gives four modes, a verified 4/5/4 NHTSA rating, an FMVSS 213a claim and 6,173 ratings.

**If you own any Maxi-Cosi Pria: always use the top tether forward-facing** — that is the exact condition under which the recalled variants failed.
