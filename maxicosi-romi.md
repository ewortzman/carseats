## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213. **No FMVSS 213a claim** on the listing.

**Official NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** (mfg. May 2022):

| Mode | Overall | Securing child | Labels | Vehicle install | Instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rear-facing | **3/5** | **2/5** | **5** | **2/5** | **5** |
| Forward-facing | **3/5** | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 |

Labels 5/5 and instructions 5/5 rear-facing are good. **2/5 for securing the child and 2/5 for vehicle-installation features rear-facing** are not.

The bigger issue is the spec, not the rating: **forward-facing caps at 40 lb and 43"**. Most seats here run to 65 lb and 49", and several to 120 lb. A child typically hits 40 lb / 43" around age 4–5, so this seat needs replacing while your child still needs a harness.

Maxi-Cosi is a **Dorel Juvenile Group** brand, as are Safety 1st and the Disney Baby car seat line. Every rated Dorel convertible or all-in-one in this comparison scores **2/5 or 3/5 rear-facing overall**: [Maxi-Cosi Romi](maxicosi-romi.md) 3/5, [Pria All-in-One](maxicosi-pria-pure-cosi.md) 3/5, [Safety 1st Grow and Go](safety1st-grow-and-go.md) 2/5, [EverSlim](safety1st-everslim.md) 2/5, [Trimate](safety1st-trimate.md) 2/5. Four of the five score **1/5 on rear-facing vehicle-installation features**.

Dorel recall history in this comparison: **21C003000** (Maxi-Cosi Pria 85, 119,863 units, potential head injury with lap-belt use, **"No remedy has been established at this time"**) and **21C004000** (Pria 70, 4,000 units, same defect — remedied with replacement labels plus a free booster seat).

## Conclusion

Don't buy this as a primary seat. The "All-in-One" name is misleading: with forward-facing limits of 40 lb and 43", it is a birth-to-preschool seat, and you will be buying another harnessed seat while your child still needs one.

At the same $149.99, [Graco TriRide](graco-triride-3in1.md) runs to 65 lb harnessed and 100 lb in booster, and NHTSA rates it 4/5 in all three modes with 5/5 instructions throughout. [Safety 1st Trimate](safety1st-trimate.md) is also $149.99 and runs to 100 lb, though it rates 2/5 rear-facing.

The Romi is defensible only as a spare seat for a young toddler in a second car.
