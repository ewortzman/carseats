## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213. **No FMVSS 213a claim** documented in this research pass — confirm on the listing and check the manufacture date sticker.

**No NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** for the Kinetic. NHTSA rates four Peg Perego seats — all **Viaggio boosters** (120 HBB, Flex 120, Shuttle 120, Shuttle Plus 120) — so there is no rated Peg Perego convertible to use as a proxy. That is a genuine evidence gap: for this brand's convertibles there is neither a federal ease-of-use score nor a meaningful owner sample.

Peg Perego brand note: 3 complaints total on the [Primo Viaggio All-in-One](pegperego-primo-viaggio-all-in-one.md) record, 0 recalls, 0 investigations. Small catalogue, small footprint.

## Conclusion

Not enough information to recommend at $349.99. Six reviews, no federal ease-of-use rating, no rated brand sibling to extrapolate from, no CSFTL teardown, and no documented 213a claim. On a purchase this consequential, that is too many blanks.

For the same money or less with real evidence: [Britax Poplar](britax-poplar.md) $307.99 (ClickTight, 50 lb rear-facing, 17", NHTSA 4/5 RF, 4.65★ with 1% one-star), [Chicco Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) $183.99 (16.7", steel lock-offs, 213a cited, zero complaints), or [Graco 4Ever DLX](graco-4ever-dlx.md) $255.99 (four modes, verified 4/5/4, 6,173 ratings).

If you want it for the build quality and Italian manufacture, at least confirm the per-mode limits and 213a status on the listing first, and get a CPST to check the harness threading out of the box — the sibling record has a factory-misthreading report.
