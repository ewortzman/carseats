## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213. **No FMVSS 213a claim** on the listing — check the manufacture date.

**Official NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** (mfg. May 2022) — the weakest profile of any seat in this comparison:

| Mode | Overall | Securing child | Labels | Vehicle install | Instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rear-facing | **2/5** | **2/5** | **1/5** | **1/5** | 3 |
| Forward-facing | **3/5** | 5 | **1/5** | 3 | 4 |
| Booster | **3/5** | **2/5** | 3 | 3 | 5 |

Read that rear-facing row carefully. **2/5 overall, 2/5 securing the child, 1/5 labels, 1/5 vehicle-installation features.** A 1/5 on install features means NHTSA found the belt paths, lock-offs, recline indicators and LATCH hardware genuinely difficult to use correctly. Labels at 1/5 in both rear- and forward-facing means the on-seat guidance is close to useless.

For contrast, [Graco TriRide](graco-triride-3in1.md) at $149.99 scores **4/5 in all three modes with 5/5 instructions throughout**.

Safety 1st and Maxi-Cosi are both **Dorel Juvenile Group** brands, as is the Disney Baby car seat line. Dorel's rated seats are the weakest group in NHTSA's dataset among the brands in this comparison: every Safety 1st and Maxi-Cosi all-in-one rated scores **2/5 or 3/5 rear-facing overall**, with **1/5 on rear-facing vehicle-installation features** on the Grow and Go, SlimRide EverSlim, Trimate and Pria All-in-One.

Dorel recall history relevant here: **21C003000** (Maxi-Cosi Pria 85, 119,863 units, potential head injury with lap-belt use, **"No remedy has been established at this time"**) and **21C004000** (Pria 70, 4,000 units, same defect, remedied with replacement labels plus a free booster seat). See [Maxi-Cosi Pria Pure Cosi](maxicosi-pria-pure-cosi.md).

The tension is worth stating plainly: a seat can be hard to install correctly (NHTSA's finding) without having a defect (the complaint record). Both facts are true here. The risk is misuse, not breakage.

## Conclusion

The honest read: this is a safe seat that is easy to use *wrong*. FMVSS 213 compliance means it protects a child who is correctly restrained in a correctly installed seat; NHTSA's 2/5 rear-facing rating means getting to that state is unusually hard, and 1/5 labels mean the seat will not help you.

If $128 is the budget, buy it — a correctly installed cheap seat beats an incorrectly installed expensive one, and the complaint record is clean. But **book a free CPST inspection before first use**, and treat the manual as the authority rather than the on-seat labels.

If you can reach $149.99, [Graco TriRide](graco-triride-3in1.md) scores 4/5 in all three modes with 5/5 instructions — a large ease-of-use upgrade for $22, though its harness is hard to tighten. At $183.99 [Chicco Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) adds steel lock-offs, an explicit 213a claim and 16.7" width.
