## Safety

Meets FMVSS 213. **No FMVSS 213a claim** on the listing.

**Official NHTSA Ease-of-Use rating** (mfg. Feb 2024):

| Mode | Overall | Securing child | Labels | Vehicle install | Instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rear-facing | **2/5** | 3 | **2/5** | **1/5** | 3 |
| Forward-facing | **3/5** | 5 | **1/5** | 3 | 3 |
| Booster | **3/5** | **2/5** | 3 | **5** | 3 |

**2/5 rear-facing overall, 1/5 rear-facing vehicle-installation features, 1/5 forward-facing labels, 2/5 securing the child in booster.** Forward-facing securing-the-child is 5/5 and booster install features are 5/5 — the harness geometry is fine; getting the seat into the car correctly is the problem.

Safety 1st and Maxi-Cosi are both **Dorel Juvenile Group** brands, as is the Disney Baby car seat line. Dorel's rated seats are the weakest group in NHTSA's dataset among the brands in this comparison: every Safety 1st and Maxi-Cosi all-in-one rated scores **2/5 or 3/5 rear-facing overall**, with **1/5 on rear-facing vehicle-installation features** on the Grow and Go, SlimRide EverSlim, Trimate and Pria All-in-One.

Dorel recall history relevant here: **21C003000** (Maxi-Cosi Pria 85, 119,863 units, potential head injury with lap-belt use, **"No remedy has been established at this time"**) and **21C004000** (Pria 70, 4,000 units, same defect, remedied with replacement labels plus a free booster seat). See [Maxi-Cosi Pria Pure Cosi](maxicosi-pria-pure-cosi.md).

## Reliability & owner feedback

4.0★ from **11 ratings** — far too few to characterise anything. Target publishes no attribute subscores.

Treat the owner data as unusable and weigh the NHTSA rating instead, which is the only substantive evidence available for this seat.

**CSFTL: unfavourable.** The reviewer places it among the very few seats she could find no best-use scenario for, describing it as **"a basic multimode seat that doesn't do anything particularly well"** and noting it is **"hard to install correctly."** Persistent side-to-side tipping, a balky recline adjuster, an inability to sit flush on the vehicle seat, and a low 17" top belt guide (so a short-lived booster stage) drove the conclusion. She could not recommend it even as a lightweight travel seat "in good conscience" — despite its **12 lb** weight, which she calls the seat's "most redeeming quality."

That is an independent expert reaching the same conclusion as NHTSA's 2/5 rear-facing rating and 1/5 install-features score. Two sources, same verdict.

## Cons

- **NHTSA rates it 2/5 rear-facing overall** with **1/5 rear-facing vehicle-installation features** and **1/5 forward-facing labels**
- **2/5 securing the child in booster mode**
- Only **11 ratings** — no meaningful owner evidence
- No FMVSS 213a claim
- Rear-facing height caps at 40"
- No published expiration date
- Does not rotate

## Conclusion

No reason to pick this over [Graco TriRide](graco-triride-3in1.md) at the same $149.99, which NHTSA rates **4/5 in all three modes with 5/5 instructions throughout** versus this seat's 2/5 rear-facing — a large, measured gap on the axis that decides whether a seat is installed correctly. The TriRide's weakness is a hard-to-tighten harness; this seat's weakness is the install itself.

At $183.99, [Chicco Fit3x](chicco-fit3x.md) is the better buy again: steel lock-offs both directions, 213a cited, 16.7", 87% would recommend.

Buy the Trimate only on a deep discount, and have a CPST install it.
